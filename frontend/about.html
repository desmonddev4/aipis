Africa Infrastructure, Prosperity and Industrialisation Summit

Why AIPIS

An annual African summit measuring whether infrastructure investment creates productive industries, jobs and better living standards.

Africa has made strong progress in raising finance for infrastructure. Governments, development finance institutions, pension funds and private investors now plan and fund projects with growing skill.

Problems remain after the funding is secured. Many projects stall, run over budget, or are completed without the industrial growth that justified them. AIPIS follows projects through construction and operation and measures what they deliver for industry and citizens.

Strategic theme, 2028 to 2032

Infrastructure that Delivers: Turning Africa’s Investment into Industry and Shared Prosperity

Every edition asks one question: has infrastructure spending in Africa produced working industries and better lives?

1

Financial close

The point at which a project’s funding is fully secured.

2

Prosperity close

The point at which a project can show measurable benefits to industry and citizens. AIPIS introduces this standard.

Linked to the Sustainable Development Goals

AIPIS is built around SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure) and contributes to SDGs 1, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 16 and 17. Every session, output and commitment is linked to SDG targets. The 2030 edition in Ghana will review progress in the SDG target year.

Every edition in Ghana

Ghana is the permanent home of AIPIS, and every edition is held in Ghana in the last week of March. The summit does not rotate between countries. Delegates, speakers and partners from across Africa come to Ghana each year, and the 2030 edition reviews progress on the Sustainable Development Goals.

Governance

Organising committees

AIPIS is governed through five bodies. The Summit Steering Committee, drawn from the three convenors, sets strategy, approves partnerships and appoints the two organising committees. The International Organising Committee leads the programme and the summit's reach across Africa and beyond. The Local Organising Committee delivers each edition in the host city. The International Advisory Board advises on direction, and an independent Scientific Committee oversees the research and practice stream.

4International Organising Committee

The committee shapes the summit programme and gives AIPIS its continental reach. It meets monthly, mostly online, in the year before each edition, and in person at the summit.

Composition

  • Chair nominated by the Steering Committee from the convenors
  • Two Vice-Chairs, one from each convenor not holding the Chair
  • Three partner-country members: one senior academic or professional each from Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa, nominated by partner universities
  • Up to seven regional members: one each from North, East, Central, West and Southern Africa, and up to two from the African diaspora
  • Government liaison nominated jointly by the Founding Government Partners
  • Top Business Brokers Consult Limited as Founding Industry Partner
  • Chairs of the Scientific and Local Organising Committees, ex officio
  • AIPIS Secretariat Secretary

Responsibilities

  • Sets the edition theme and session topics under the strategic theme
  • Identifies and invites Distinguished Keynote Speakers, Special Guest Speakers and Technical Keynote Presenters
  • Promotes the call for papers, posters and Forensic Clinic cases across Africa and internationally
  • Secures international partners, development finance institutions and delegates
  • Oversees the Prosperity Index, the Unfinished Projects Forensic Report and the Commitments Tracker for each edition
  • Builds links with partner universities, governments and financiers across Africa

5Local Organising Committee

The committee delivers each edition from the AIPIS Secretariat at KNUST, working with partners in the host city.

Composition

  • Chair nominated by the Steering Committee from KNUST (CABE and DCTM)
  • Vice-Chair nominated from AIPAF or GCI
  • AIPIS Secretariat Secretary and Finance Officer
  • Chairs of the nine sub-committees
  • Accra Metropolitan Assembly and Greater Accra Regional Coordinating Council representatives for 2027
  • Liaison for traditional authorities and student volunteer coordinator
